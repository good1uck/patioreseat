A replacement cushion is a custom-ish object even when it is sold as a standard SKU. The goal of measuring is not to produce a pretty number. It is to produce a written record you can compare to a factory part, a retailer listing, or a custom-mill order form.

## What you need

- A rigid or quality retractable tape measure marked in inches
- A notepad or phone note you will not lose
- A flat floor or table
- Daylight and two or three photos: the whole chair, the cushion off the frame, and any label or decal

Write every number down twice. If the two readings disagree, measure a third time.

## 1. Identify the collection if you can

Before you unroll the tape, look for a name. Many brands put a paper or fabric tag on a cushion zipper, or a metal / foil decal on the frame.

[Homecrest](/brands/homecrest/) says products manufactured after 1998 carry a manufacturing decal on the metal frame: the first two digits are the week of the year, the middle digits are the model number, and the last two digits are the year of production ([Homecrest FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default)). That model number is how Homecrest wants you to order an official replacement.

[Brown Jordan](/brands/brown-jordan/) does not ask you to decode a public size chart. Its FAQ says you may email a photo and your name to `bjcservice@brownjordan.com` so the company can name the collection, and that replacement cushions are purchased from a local retailer ([Brown Jordan FAQ](https://www.brownjordan.com/support/faq)).

[Crate & Barrel](/brands/crate-and-barrel/) official replacements are titled with the collection name — for example, a cushion “designed especially for our Batten outdoor dining chair” ([Batten replacement product page](https://www.crateandbarrel.com/replacement-batten-canvas-sunbrella-outdoor-side-chair-cushion/s589423)). If you still have the original order or a hangtag, that name is the shortest path.

If you cannot identify the collection, you can still order a custom cushion. You just cannot skip the next steps.

## 2. Decide what you are actually replacing

There are two different jobs:

- **Cover only**, when the foam still springs back and is not crumbling or waterlogged, but the fabric has faded, torn, or mildewed on the surface.
- **Full cushion**, when the pad has gone flat, flattened in a sitting well, or soaked up water and become heavy.

A beautiful new cover over dead foam will still sit badly. If you are unsure, press the cushion and see whether it rebounds; also weigh it in your hands against an identical piece that has lived indoors, if you have one. We do not publish “average lifespan” figures here — use the condition in front of you.

## 3. Measure the cushion, not the catalog

This is the rule the rest of this site is built on.

Furniture catalogs often list overall **width, depth, height, seat height, and arm height**. Those numbers describe the frame. They are useful for knowing whether a sofa will fit a porch. They are not a replacement-cushion size.

Brown Jordan’s public FAQ does not include a factory cushion-size chart; official cushions are sold through retailers ([FAQ](https://www.brownjordan.com/support/faq)). If you later see a third-party table of “published sizes,” treat those as another shop’s reference, not as a measurement of *your* pad. Always prefer the tape.

**How to measure a removable cushion:**

1. Take the cushion off the furniture.
2. Place it on a flat surface, fabric side up, in the shape it would have when sitting (do not stretch a floppy cover).
3. **Width** is left to right as you sit.
4. **Depth** (sometimes called length on an order form) is front to back on a seat, or bottom to top on a back.
5. **Thickness** is the height of the foam at the front edge of a seat, and at the lower edge of a back, unless the shop asks for a finished, crowned height.

If the cushion is a rounded “waterfall” front (no seam on the leading edge), say so in your notes. Crate & Barrel, for example, describes its Mallorca sofa replacement as using “waterfall construction” ([Mallorca sofa cushion page](https://www.crateandbarrel.com/replacement-mallorca-canvas-white-sunbrella-outdoor-sofa-cushion/s518752)). That is a construction detail, not a size, but it changes how a cover is sewn.

If the original cushion is missing, measure the **platform** you will sit on: the slats, straps, or sling deck, left to right and front to back. Then tell the mill “this is the frame opening, not a finished cushion.” A finished seat is often a little larger than the open deck so it does not look undersized; how much larger is a pattern decision the mill should confirm. We will not invent an ease allowance.

## 4. Measure seat and back separately

Lounge chairs, sofas, and loveseats often use a seat *and* a back (sometimes plus arms). Dining pads may be a single seat. Chaises may be one long piece with a fold, or two pieces.

Make a separate line in your notebook for every physical piece you will order:

- Dining seat
- Lounge seat
- Lounge back
- Ottoman
- Chaise (and whether it is one piece or two)

If two seat cushions sit side by side on a loveseat, measure **one** of them, then count how many you need. Do not measure the whole loveseat width and assume the mill will divide it.

## 5. Corners, ties, zippers, and odd shapes

Sketch a bird’s-eye outline. Then note:

- **Square corners** or **rounded corners**. If rounded, measure the corner radius if you can (a compass or a round household object of known diameter helps). If you cannot measure a radius confidently, write “rounded, see photo” and attach a picture with a tape in the frame.
- **Boxed** (a visible sidewall, like a layer cake) versus **knife-edge** (front seam comes to a line) versus **waterfall**.
- **Welt / piping** (a cord in the seam). Homecrest defines a welt in its FAQ in the context of slings, as trim attached with extra stitching ([Homecrest FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default)). On cushions, the idea is the same: say whether you want a welt, and whether it should match or contrast.
- **Ties, Velcro, or a non-slip bottom**, and where they sit (rear corners of a dining pad are typical).
- **Zipper placement**. Some official cushions put a ventilation zipper on the underside, as Crate & Barrel notes on the Batten dining seat ([product page](https://www.crateandbarrel.com/replacement-batten-canvas-sunbrella-outdoor-side-chair-cushion/s589423)).

Curved sectional wedges, kidney-shaped backs, and cut-outs around arms are not jobs for a single width × depth number. Photograph them with the tape visible and tell the mill you need a pattern from photos and measurements.

## 6. Use an official replacement path when one exists — and still keep your numbers

If Homecrest publishes a replacement number for your model, start there: you should not have to guess a size for an authentic factory cushion ([Homecrest FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default), [Model ID & Replacement Guide PDF](https://www.homecrest.com/documents/model_id_guide.pdf)). If Crate & Barrel still sells a “Replacement [collection]” SKU for your exact piece, that is the most direct official path. If Brown Jordan refers you to a retailer, take the collection name and your photos to that retailer.

Keep your measurements anyway. They are the only way to notice a mismatch before you unbox a pad that does not sit in the frame.

## A simple record you can reuse

Copy this onto a card, one card per piece:

- Brand / collection / model number (or “unknown”)
- Piece type (seat, back, ottoman, chaise…)
- Width: ____ in
- Depth: ____ in
- Thickness: ____ in
- Corners: square / rounded (radius ____ )
- Construction: boxed / knife-edge / waterfall / other
- Ties or zipper: ____
- Photo filenames: ____
- Date measured: ____
