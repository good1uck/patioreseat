Most replacement-cushion conversations jump straight to a brand name — “just make it in Sunbrella.” The useful question is more specific: what is the *fiber*, what did the mill actually claim, and is it the cover, the foam, or both that failed?

This page sticks to statements we can attribute. It is not a lab test and not a medical or chemical safety review.

## Solution-dyed acrylic, in the mill’s words

Sunbrella’s “Fabric Difference” page states that its performance fabrics are made of **100% solution-dyed acrylic**. Color and UV-stabilized pigments are saturated into the fibers *before* those fibers are spun into yarn, “meaning the pigment goes all the way through the fibers and the color stays true over time.” The same page says this process makes the fabrics “inherently fade resistant, UV protective and durable to the core.”

Sunbrella also contrasts that construction with fabrics that depend on a **surface finish** for stain resistance: “Complete performance does not rely on a finish which can wear off. Sunbrella engineers performance into the core of each fiber, including stain-resistant technology.”

On durability claims, the same page says the fibers are “mold and mildew resistant, stain and water resistant.” Those are manufacturer claims. They do not mean a cushion can be left in a puddle, and they do not mean mildew will never appear on dirt that sits on the surface.

Source: [Sunbrella Fabric Difference](https://www.sunbrella.com/sunbrella-fabric-difference).

## Water-resistant is not the same as a dry interior

A cover can be water-repellent or stain-resistant and still hide foam that has already taken on water. Construction details matter:

- [Crate & Barrel’s Batten dining-seat replacement](https://www.crateandbarrel.com/replacement-batten-canvas-sunbrella-outdoor-side-chair-cushion/s589423) is described as an all-weather Sunbrella cover made with solution-dyed acrylic fibers, “fade-resistant, water-repellent and stain-resistant,” with a polyfoam insert and an “underside ventilation zipper.”
- [Crate & Barrel’s Mallorca sofa replacement](https://www.crateandbarrel.com/replacement-mallorca-canvas-white-sunbrella-outdoor-sofa-cushion/s518752) is listed as “100% acrylic weather-resistant fabric,” with a “polyfoam core,” “poly-fiber fill and wrap,” and “Texteline drainage panels.”

We are not generalizing those construction details to every outdoor cushion. We are pointing at them because they show what a careful product page actually discloses: **fabric fiber, fill, and drainage are separate specifications.** If a custom quote lists only a fabric name, ask about the foam and about drainage or a vented zipper.

## Care, as written in Sunbrella’s own guides

Sunbrella publishes PDF care instructions. The upholstery guide’s opening advice is to brush off dirt before it becomes embedded, and to deal with spills promptly. For a fresh spill it tells you to:

- **Blot, don’t rub**, liquid with a clean, dry cloth
- For oil-based spills, apply an absorbent such as cornstarch, then remove it with a straight edge
- Use a mild soap-and-water solution or Sunbrella Clean, then **rinse thoroughly** to remove soap residue
- **Air dry**

The same guide says to avoid scrubbing and hard-bristle brushes, and that “Sunbrella should only be allowed to air dry.” It also says not to use a steamer or an iron on a steam setting if you are smoothing wrinkles (a dry iron on a synthetic setting is discussed, with a test in an inconspicuous area first).

Some removable casings, the guide says, can be machine washed — but you should **consult the furniture manufacturer** first. The listed method is cold water, delicate cycle, mild detergent, then air dry. For severe mold or mildew on those removable casings, the guide mentions adding bleach, with cautions: protect surrounding non-Sunbrella materials, rinse thoroughly, and follow the bleach label, because bleach can harm nearby plants and surfaces.

Sunbrella’s marine-care guide adds a useful distinction: the fabrics “do not promote mildew growth,” but “mildew may grow on dirt and other foreign substances that are not removed from the fabric.”

Sources: [Sunbrella Upholstery Care and Cleaning (PDF)](https://www.sunbrella.com/media/pdf/sunbrella-upholstery-care-cleaning-en-us.pdf), [Sunbrella Cleaning Guide (PDF)](https://www.sunbrella.com/media/pdf/sunbrella-cleaning-guide-en-us.pdf), [Sunbrella Marine Canvas Care and Cleaning (PDF)](https://www.sunbrella.com/media/pdf/sunbrella-marine-canvas-care-cleaning-en-us.pdf).

If your cushion cover is *not* a Sunbrella fabric, do not borrow the bleach step. Follow the care card that came with that fabric.

## Other names you will see, and what we will not invent

Furniture brands often use their own trademarked fabric names on top of a fiber type.

- Brown Jordan describes some collections as using **Suncloth** — for example, its collections page says John Caldwell created the Softscape Cushion and Softscape Strap collections in 2018 “using revolutionary new Suncloth fabric” ([Brown Jordan collections](https://www.brownjordan.com/products/collection)). We have not restated Suncloth’s fiber content here because that belongs on Brown Jordan’s own fabric documentation. Brown Jordan sells both a Suncloth *fabric* (for cushions) and a Suncloth *strap*; our [Softscape guide](/brands/brown-jordan/softscape/) explains the difference and how fabric numbers and grades appear in the catalog.
- Homecrest describes **Sensation** fabrics as a sling material that “combine[s] the strength and UV-stability of PVC fibers with the comfort and outstanding memory of elastomeric yarns,” and as exclusive to Homecrest ([Homecrest FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default)). That is a sling story, not automatically a boxed-cushion story. Homecrest also states on its About page that it “was also the first outdoor furniture manufacturer to use Sunbrella fabrics on outdoor furniture” ([About Homecrest](https://www.homecrest.com/About/default)).

We do not publish fade-year ratings, warranty lengths, or “best fabric” scores unless a primary source states them on the page we are citing. Warranties also change; read the current document from the mill or the furniture brand.

## A practical buying order

1. Confirm whether you need a **cover, a full cushion, or an official factory replacement** ([how to measure](/guides/how-to-measure/)).
2. If the brand still sells an official SKU in a named fabric, that is the cleanest color match to the rest of the set.
3. If you are going custom, ask the mill for the **fiber type** (for example, solution-dyed acrylic), the **fabric brand and color code**, and the **foam specification** in writing.
4. Keep a swatch or a cutting from the old cover, because screen colors lie.
