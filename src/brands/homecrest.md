Homecrest Outdoor Living began in 1953 in Wadena, Minnesota, when Mert Bottemiller and Al Engelmann started building hassocks (ottomans) out of a retail furniture shop and then expanded into indoor and outdoor furniture. The company states that Bottemiller’s swivel-rocker mechanism was patented in 1956, that furniture is still made in Wadena, and that Homecrest “was also the first outdoor furniture manufacturer to use Sunbrella fabrics on outdoor furniture” ([About Homecrest](https://www.homecrest.com/About/default); [Homecrest FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default)).

For replacement cushions, Homecrest’s official message is the opposite of “guess a size”: identify the **model**, then order an authentic factory part.

## The official replacement path

The FAQ, under “Where can I get replacement slings/cushions for my furniture?”, tells you to locate an authorized dealer **or** [shop replacements online](https://www.homecrest.com/replacements/default). A separate question, “Can I buy direct from Homecrest Outdoor Living?”, applies to buying furniture — Homecrest says it relies on authorized dealers so you can see products in person ([FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default)).

Homecrest publishes two identification tools:

- The [Catalog Archive](https://www.homecrest.com/catalog-archive/default), which the company calls “an invaluable tool for identifying replacements… by allowing you to look through past catalogs.”
- The [Model Identification Guide (PDF)](https://www.homecrest.com/documents/model_id_guide.pdf), also linked from the replacements area. The FAQ says you can search the guide by model number, collection name, year of purchase, and product type, then use the “Replacement #” column when you order.

**If your furniture was made after 1998**, look on the metal frame for a manufacturing decal. Homecrest says the first two digits are the week of the year, the next set is the model number, and the last two digits are the year of production. Use that model number in the identification guide ([FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default)).

That official path is why this page does **not** invent cushion dimensions. If you are ordering a factory replacement by model number, Homecrest’s system is built so you should not have to invent them either. If you are instead going to a custom mill — for example, because a style has been discontinued without a listed successor — then [measure the cushion](/guides/how-to-measure/) and say you are *not* using a Homecrest replacement number.

The Model ID guide itself also discusses discontinued cushion styles and successor styles in places. Read the current PDF rather than relying on a restated table here; successor mappings can change.

## Cushion collections listed on the official site

Homecrest’s [Cushion collections](https://www.homecrest.com/collections/cushion) page, checked on November 18, 2026 (America/New_York), listed the following names. Frame material is included only where the page stated it. This is a snapshot, not a promise that every collection is still in production tomorrow.

- Anthem Cushion — aluminum
- Ava Cushion — all-weather poly
- Blair Cushion — aluminum
- Blair Slip Cover
- Boden Cushion — aluminum
- Elements Cushion
- Elements Modular
- Holly Hill Cushion
- Liberty Cushion
- Revive Cushion
- Revive Modular
- Urban Cushion
- Wells Cushion — all-weather poly / aluminum

If your set is one of these, you still want the **model number** (from the decal, archive, or dealer), not just the collection name. Two pieces in the same collection can use different replacement numbers.

The About page also says the product line is “offered in 30 collections,” including tables, chairs, chaises, loveseats, sofas, ottomans, and bar or balcony seating, in aluminum, steel, and cast frames ([About Homecrest](https://www.homecrest.com/About/default)). Collection counts on marketing pages can lag the catalog; use the archive and the Model ID guide for identification.

## Slings, padded slings, and slip covers are not the same as boxed cushions

Homecrest sells more than one kind of seating surface. The FAQ spends a lot of time on **slings** and **padded slings**, including Sensation sling fabrics (PVC fibers plus elastomeric yarns, described as exclusive to Homecrest) and welting ([FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default)). **Blair Slip Cover**, listed on the cushion collections page, is a different construction from a standard boxed cushion.

When you contact a dealer or use the replacements shop, say which of these you have. A sling tool and a cushion order are not interchangeable jobs.

## If you cannot find a model number

The FAQ walks through visual checks mainly for **sling baskets** (external vs. internal cross bars, and high-back vs. low-back measurements in inches). That section is about slings, not boxed cushions. If you are still stuck, Homecrest says you can visit a retailer with a picture and some measurements, or connect with the company online ([FAQ](https://www.homecrest.com/faq/default)).

For a custom, non-factory cushion, follow [how to measure](/guides/how-to-measure/) and our [fabric guide](/guides/fabrics/).
