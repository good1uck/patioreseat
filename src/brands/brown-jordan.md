Brown Jordan is a luxury outdoor-furniture brand founded in 1945 in Pasadena, California, by Robert Brown and Hubert Jordan ([Brown Jordan, “Modern Heritage in Montecito”](https://www.brownjordan.com/news/modern-heritage-in-montecito); [“80 Years of Timeless Design”](https://www.brownjordan.com/news/celebrating-80-years)).

If you have a Brown Jordan frame and tired pads, start with the company’s own replacement path — not a guessed size chart.

## The official replacement path

Brown Jordan’s FAQ answers “How do I purchase replacement mesh slings, cushions or strap for my Brown Jordan furniture?” as follows:

- **Replacement slings and strap** can be purchased from a Brown Jordan retailer or a local service technician.
- **Replacement cushions** can be purchased from your local Brown Jordan retailer.

It points you to the [Locations](https://www.brownjordan.com/locations) page and to a technician page in the site’s Resources section ([Brown Jordan FAQ](https://www.brownjordan.com/support/faq)).

The same FAQ does **not** publish a factory cushion-size chart. We therefore do not list cushion dimensions on this page. If you find overall width, depth, and height in a catalog, those figures describe the furniture, not the pad. Measure the cushion itself using our [how-to-measure guide](/guides/how-to-measure/).

## If you do not know the collection name

The FAQ invites you to email a **photo of the furniture and your name** to `bjcservice@brownjordan.com`. “We would be happy to provide you with the name of your Brown Jordan collection.” That is the official identification route ([FAQ](https://www.brownjordan.com/support/faq)).

Take the collection name, your photos, and — if you have it — proof of purchase to a retailer when you ask about official cushions. Warranty claims, according to the same FAQ, also run through a retailer and require proof of purchase and photos; read the current [claims and warranty](https://www.brownjordan.com/support/faq) information on the brand site before you assume coverage.

## Current collections that are described with cushions

The following notes are paraphrased from Brown Jordan’s public [All Collections](https://www.brownjordan.com/products/collection) page, checked on November 18, 2026 (America/New_York). This is not a complete historical catalog, and it is not a stock list. Names can move on and off the page.

- **[Palmetto](#palmetto).** The collections page describes sculpted aluminum frames, tailored cushions, and a distinctive cane sling, “inspired by the relaxed elegance of the Low Country.”
- **[Fremont](#fremont).** Designed by John Caldwell. “The collection is available as Fremont Cushion and Fremont Sling.”
- **[Pasadena](#pasadena).** “In 2017, award-winning designer John Caldwell created Pasadena Cushion and Pasadena Sling, collections that nod to the brand’s mid-century California roots and 80-years expertise in forged aluminum.”
- **[Softscape](#softscape).** “In 2018, designer John Caldwell created the simple, luxurious Softscape Cushion and Softscape Strap collections using revolutionary new Suncloth fabric.”
- **[Parkway](#parkway).** Designed by John Caldwell. The Parkway collection page names “three material translations, Parkway Sling, Parkway Cushion, and Parkway Flex,” plus a later Modular system.
- **Sonora.** Described as usable “with or without a slim cushion,” with a sling base.
- **Vero.** Described as modular, with “generous comfort.” The collections page does not, in the text we checked, publish cushion sizes.
- **Solenne.** Teak and powder-coated aluminum, “enduring comfort” — again, no cushion-size chart in that write-up.
- **Huntley.** “Designed with modular versatility in mind.”
- **Juno** poufs, described as pairing woven rope with “waterproof, high-density cushioning” and an aluminum frame.
- **Ventura** poufs, “thoughtfully tailored” for use as an ottoman, extra seat, or casual surface.

Older or heritage names also appear on the same page and in the brand’s anniversary article, including **Calcutta** (originally designed by Hall Bradley in 1967, reintroduced by John Caldwell in 1987), **Sol Y Luna** (first released in 1954, described as an adaptation of Dan Johnson’s Gazelle Collection in cast aluminum), **Still** (Richard Frinier, 2012), and **Venetian** (the anniversary page lists “Since 1987 – Venetian Collection”). We mention them because owners of older sets often search these names. We still do not have official cushion dimensions to publish for them.

If your piece is a sling or strap design rather than a boxed cushion, say so when you contact a retailer. Those are different replacement parts on the FAQ.

### Palmetto

Aluminum frames with tailored cushions and a cane sling, in the brand’s own description. If you are ordering replacements, confirm whether you need the **cushion**, the **cane sling**, or both — the collections copy mentions all three. See our full [Palmetto replacement guide](/brands/brown-jordan/palmetto/).

### Fremont

Order the official path as **Fremont Cushion** or **Fremont Sling**, which the brand treats as related but distinct. Do not assume a sling pattern will cut as a boxed cushion, or the reverse. See our full [Fremont Cushion vs. Fremont Sling guide](/brands/brown-jordan/fremont/).

### Pasadena

A John Caldwell collection with both Cushion and Sling versions, described as a nod to mid-century California and the company’s aluminum work. Brown Jordan’s catalog also lists a **Padded Sling** group inside Pasadena Sling, and only Pasadena Cushion uses loose cushions. A retailer will want the exact name (Pasadena Cushion, Padded Sling, or Sling). See our full [Pasadena replacement guide](/brands/brown-jordan/pasadena/).

### Softscape

A 2018 John Caldwell collection in Cushion (5350-) and Strap (5340-) versions, described as using Suncloth fabric. Most Softscape Cushion seating also has a Suncloth strap on the frame, so work out whether the worn part is a cushion or a strap before you order. See our full [Softscape Cushion vs. Strap guide](/brands/brown-jordan/softscape/), which also explains Suncloth Fabric vs. Suncloth Strap. For fabric background in general, see our [fabric guide](/guides/fabrics/).

### Parkway

A John Caldwell collection in four builds: Parkway Cushion (3470-), Parkway Modular (4560-), Parkway Sling (5330-), and Parkway Framed Sling, which the 2024 catalog calls Parkway Flex Sling and which also uses 3470- numbers. Only Cushion and Modular use loose cushions, so give a retailer the full item number, not just the last four digits. See our full [Parkway replacement guide](/brands/brown-jordan/parkway/).

## When a custom mill is the backup

If a retailer cannot supply a pad — a retired SKU, a single missing piece, or a fabric the official line no longer carries — a custom shop can cut to **your measurements**. That is a backup, not a shortcut around identification. Photograph the frame, keep any official collection name you received from customer service, and measure with the [guide](/guides/how-to-measure/).
